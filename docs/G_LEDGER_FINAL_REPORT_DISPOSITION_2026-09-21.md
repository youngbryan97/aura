The reference package is the previously reviewed five-test toy implementation.
Its passes establish those fixtures, not repair of Aura's 24 current failures.
The report's opening says it resolves the bottlenecks; its final matrix leaves
them open. The latter is consistent with the available evidence.

## Accepted directions

- Separate label integrity, representation, constraints, reachability, ranking
  and public emission. The feasibility audit and boundary interventions now
  provide additional evidence at these boundaries.
- Keep full development evaluation separate from untouched transfer. Preregister
  the fresh experiment before collecting its outcomes, not afterward as the
  page-six dependency diagram could imply.
- Use deployed decision rules in learning, mine newly winning competitors and
  preserve numerical replay. Existing first-feasible policy, graph retention,
  capacity certificates and fixed-bank replay remain the implementation owners.
- Continue cross-backend execution, knowledge revision and outcome learning
  independently of compiler perfection. Reuse existing procedure, evidence,
  registry and value-of-computation contracts.
- Measure full public answers and causal lesions under matched information,
  tool access and compute accounting. Retain all failures and uncertainty.
- Qualify exact model/geometry, rollback and serving behavior before promotion.
  The proposed latency and accuracy thresholds require rationale and measured
  host feasibility; they are not inherited acceptance constants.

## Claims not adopted

1. Lexicographic selection already exists. The 492/500 prediction, claimed
   eight-error repair, and sub-150 ms runtime are not measurements of the current
   candidate. Normalized joint scoring changes decisions and needs evaluation.
2. Failure of an unregularized MLP to fit does not prove a lack of linearly
   separable information. Optimization, architecture and numerics remain possible
   causes. Exact collisions or a valid certificate for a declared score class
   support stronger conclusions.
3. A bounded-margin LP with optimum at zero does not always imply contradictory
   labels: a strict correct ordering can exist only at margins smaller than the
   imposed requirement or parameter bound. Feasibility also does not establish
   statistical generalization, and solver status alone is not an exact proof.
4. The zero-regression necessity argument assumes joint positive probability
   from two positive marginals. They can be disjoint: baseline correctness may
   imply candidate correctness even though the candidate sometimes fails when
   the baseline also fails. Baseline retention alone still gives no guarantee.
5. Integer-search pruning does not make general integer programming polynomial.
   The quoted 70 percent reduction and per-chart timings need measurements.
6. Well-typed execution proves properties of the represented program only with
   valid primitive preconditions and composition rules. It does not certify user
   intent. A floor execution must not automatically overrule a correct model answer.
7. Unmeasured emission/execution error cannot be set to zero in a union bound.
   The illustrative one-percent bound is not an end-to-end Aura error bound.
8. Sample size 384 is not established by the stated paired three-point effect
   and eight-percent discordance. Use the existing powered-plan implementation
   with declared alternatives, clustering and stopping rules.
9. The named 2024/2025 baseline snapshots are not evidence of current frontier
   status in September 2026. Verify primary sources and actual access before G12.
10. A fixed 50-percent failure-stage rule does not imply that new primitives or
    a particular objective are needed. Diagnose the missing information or
    mechanism before selecting the intervention.

No G checkpoint closes from this report. Its valid proposals map to the existing
review disposition Q01-Q38 and the feasibility audit; no second compiler,
arbitrator or knowledge store is justified by the report alone.
