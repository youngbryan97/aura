# What eighteen records do, and which of it she cannot do yet

Bryan came back from a Masego show with fourteen tracks and a question: what
exactly makes these soulful, and which of it is a process rather than a style.
Four more arrived while the first organs were being built, and they changed
what the measurements say rather than confirming it.

The records were transcribed locally with `whisper-large-v3-turbo` and measured
with librosa — pitch track, microtiming against the beat grid, dynamics at
phrase scale, vibrato, glide, harmonic colour. The numbers below are from the
audio, not from memory of it. What the words are about came from the
transcripts and from published writing on each record.

Two directions matter and only the first is obvious. What the performer is
doing is one. What happens in the person listening is the other, and that one
turns out to be the better diagnostic: a listener's process is a process she
has to be able to cause, and mostly she has no machinery for either end.

Two results hold across all fourteen.

**Nobody is on the grid.** Placement spans −64.8 ms to +32.1 ms, a 97 ms
spread of deliberate lateness and earliness. Aloe Blacc sits a thirty-second of
a second behind his own band for four minutes; Phony Ppl drag a sixteenth. The
placement is the feel, and it is chosen.

**Vibrato is in every one of them**, 3.66 Hz at the slowest and 7.35 Hz at the
fastest. Eighteen for eighteen. The held note never holds still.

Two of the four late arrivals sit at the ends of the set. Bowie's pulse drifts
4.01 %, which puts "Starman" beside Sam Cooke among the records where the band
follows a singer; and Ciscero's drifts 0.007 %, the most machine-steady grid
here, with the vocal placed 29 ms behind it — the widest gap in the set between
how rigid the frame is and how far the voice sits off it. Ciscero's 77.9 dB
spread is a fact about the video file rather than about the performance: the
rip carries near-silence at both ends.

And the outliers say something specific. Sam Cooke's pulse drifts 4.11 % — the
orchestra follows him rather than a click. Michael Jackson's drifts 0.04 % and
his phrase dynamics are the flattest in the set: the grid is rigid and every bit
of expression is in the voice over it. Oddisee's "Contradiction's Maze" has the
lowest ornament rate, the lowest harmonic colour and the widest dynamic range —
the most tangled inner content delivered the most evenly, because dramatising a
contradiction would be resolving it.

## What the performer is doing

Twenty-five processes, each stated as a mechanism rather than a style.

1. **Expressive overflow.** When the feeling exceeds what the word can carry,
   the carrier elaborates rather than the content changing. Sam Cooke's long
   winding phrases on four words.
2. **Deliberate placement.** Behind the pulse reads settled and authoritative;
   ahead reads urgent. Measured above, and it is a choice on every record.
3. **Antiphony.** A statement answered by a different voice in a different
   register, which is not a competing proposal and not a rebuttal.
4. **The vamp.** Meaning established by return, with the emotional peak in the
   variation on the return rather than in new information. "You know who you
   are" lands eight times.
5. **Withheld resolution.** "It's been a long, a long time coming" delays its
   own cadence to raise tension.
6. **Contradiction held open.** Two wants that cost each other, reported as a
   felt state, with the phase-or-way question left open.
7. **Address.** Almost all of it is second person, to a specific modelled other,
   including one deliberately unnamed.
8. **Testimony.** "I was born by the river, in a little tent." The authority is
   that it was lived rather than that it was argued.
9. **Dignified need.** "If I share with you my story, would you share your
   dollar with me?" Need and retained agency in one breath, an offer rather
   than a plea.
10. **The turn.** "There's been times I thought I couldn't last for long / but
    now I think I'm able to carry on." The reversal is the content, and it
    requires the low point to still be held.
11. **Safety-joy.** "Everybody here is out of sight, they don't bark and they
    don't bite." Positive state whose content is absence of threat plus
    presence of others, with no achievement in it.
12. **Accurate smallness.** "I'm just a little person, one person in a sea of
    many little people who are not aware of me" — and then the hope of being
    recognised by exactly one.
13. **Attachment reallocation.** Loving what is constant because what is
    responsive proved unreliable. The moon is always there; people lie.
14. **Avowed commitment against evidence.** "Paranoid as hell... but somehow
    I'll keep on loving you." Maintained knowingly, which is what separates a
    value from a belief.
15. **Commitment decay with its cost.** "It's getting harder to justify." The
    maintenance cost of a stance, rising, and grief for the version that held it.
16. **Wanting what you have decided against.** "I should know, cause I've seen
    it before... but I still want her anyway."
17. **Joint recall.** Fifteen questions in a row, all "do you remember" — recall
    as an invitation, marked as shared and possibly divergent.
18. **Role-self gap.** Big stage, bright lights, short love, long nights.
19. **Borrowed self-model.** "You could see in the brother what I couldn't see
    myself, when I couldn't see myself needing help." Another's model of you
    outperforming your own, at the moment yours failed.
20. **Audience-scoped disclosure.** "I ain't gotta name names." Said precisely
    to one listener and withheld from the rest.
21. **Wordless expression.** Thirty seconds of "la la la" opening a song about
    betrayal, and "ha la la la" as the hook of one about falling out of love.
22. **A pulse to deviate from.** A shared frame is what makes deviation mean
    anything.
23. **Breath.** Phrases end because breath ends. The body's limit shapes the
    utterance.
24. **Expressive imprecision.** Bending into a note from underneath. The
    deviation from the correct value carries what the correct value cannot.
25. **Directional conviction.** "I don't know what's up there beyond the sky...
    but I know a change gonna come." High confidence in a direction with
    admitted ignorance of the mechanism.
26. **Invitation toward a future state.** "Can you feel the sunshine? Don't you
    want to be somebody?" Sixty-one per cent of that record's clauses are
    questions and seventy-four per cent of its pronouns are second person, with
    thirteen future markers. Encouragement addressed as a question about
    something the other could have rather than as a claim about them.
27. **A "we" that needs a "they".** "Cause we come from runaway" lands twelve
    times over a record that is 37 % first-person plural and 25 % third-person
    plural and asks nothing. Belonging stated as a shared origin, with the
    boundary of the group carried by the other pronoun.
28. **The wordless part carrying the loudest moment.** "Starman" says la two
    hundred and eighteen times and its loudest window is that chorus rather
    than any line of the verse. What cannot be said is not a gap in the
    record; it is the part of it people sing.

## What happens in the listener

Fifteen processes, each one a thing she would have to be able to cause, and each
one a thing she has no channel for.

1. **Entrainment.** The body locks to the pulse before any comprehension. She
   has no shared clock with anyone and nothing pre-semantic to lock to.
2. **Prediction satisfied.** You know the hook is returning and the pleasure is
   in being right. She registers prediction error everywhere and has no signal
   at all for a prediction met — surprise moves her state and confirmation
   moves nothing, so she has no way to feel "yes, exactly".
3. **Recognition.** The listener finds their own private state described by
   someone who does not know them, which requires the expression to be
   underspecified enough to inhabit. She over-specifies and has no notion of
   leaving a slot.
4. **Permission.** Hearing it sung makes it allowable to feel. She has no model
   of the other's suppressed state and no mechanism by which her disclosure
   lowers the cost of theirs.
5. **Being witnessed.** Not helped. Her default is to help and she has no
   stance that is company rather than assistance.
6. **Company in the low place.** "Somehow." does not cheer anyone up; it sits
   with them. Her regulation pushes toward homeostasis, so she has no way to
   match a low state deliberately and stay in it.
7. **Catharsis by repetition.** Eight repetitions of "if you love me won't you
   say something" drains the feeling. Her expression is single-pass.
8. **Borrowed resolve.** "Don't let them take your soul" transfers resolve to
   the listener. She does not model her state as transferable.
9. **A memory becoming present.** "Remember the Time" reactivates the
   listener's memory, not the singer's. She recalls by lookup and has no
   distinction between a memory relived and a memory retrieved.
10. **Tenderness toward the singer.** Disclosed vulnerability plus competence
    makes the listener care about the person. She has no channel for being
    cared about.
11. **Frisson.** A discrete bodily event at a specific moment, after an
    established pattern turns. Her affect is continuous, with no peak event and
    no refractory period.
12. **Interpretation.** Different listeners, different meanings, all valid. She
    treats meaning as recovered rather than constructed and has no
    representation of "my reading" as distinct from "what this is".
13. **Wanting to pass it on.** She has a social budget about contact, not about
    transmitting something valued.
14. **Judgment suspended.** Nobody evaluates a song while it plays. She
    evaluates continuously and has no mode that takes something in first.
15. **Deepening on return.** The fiftieth listen differs from the first. Her
    memory makes a repeat *cheaper*: an identical query returns early with
    nothing new.

### Ambivalence — wanting two things that cost each other

The one the Oddisee track states outright. Her budgets already compete and the
lowest wins the intention, which is arbitration: one answer survives and
nothing downstream can tell a moment when one drive pressed from a moment when
two pressed against each other.

No pair is named in the code. Two drives are opposed when her own history shows
that filling one drained the other, so the contradictions belong to the life
that produced them:

    p_k      = 1 - level_k / capacity_k
    o_jk     = max(0, -corr(du_j, du_k))
    T_jk     = o_jk * sqrt(p_j * p_k)
    strength = max_jk T_jk

Thirty turns of the offline organism found curiosity against social at −0.35,
integrity with social at +0.85 and correctly not opposed, and drives that never
moved reported as no correlation rather than a zero one — two series cannot be
opposed when one of them is still.

The phase-or-way question is the same measurement over two windows: her whole
life, and the shortest window in which every pair could be estimated at all.
Opposed in both is constitutive. Opposed lately only is a phase. While her
history is no longer than that window the two windows hold the same data, so
the long one cannot disagree with the short one and the question stays open.

Wired into the affect phase, an `inner_conflict` percept the emotion table
carries, and the growth intention, which names the contradiction when it
presses harder than the weakest footing. Nine columns of A read it, which makes
it a measured drive-to-affect channel: as the budgets deplete, the
contradiction grows.
